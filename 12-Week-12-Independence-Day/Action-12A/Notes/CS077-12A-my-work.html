How the three hats work
  • Visual: sketch what the player sees. A slide or image editor is enough.
  • Logical: name actions, methods, conditions and the order they run.

C# reminder / use last week’s knowledge

Class: a type describing data and behavior. Object: one instance. Field/property: data. Method: an action. Condition: a decision. Reference: a connection to a particular object. Use the names from your actual project when explaining these.

Locate the scene, the player, the behavior file/unit, the method/event and a value it reads or changes. Trace the order aloud. If you cannot explain a borrowed block, investigate it before treating it as your solution.

Required AI / ask, check and explain

Prompt: “I am completing a C# game-development mini-pipeline in [engine/version]. My intended result is [result]. My relevant code or event flow is [small excerpt]. Explain the class/object, actions and data involved; help me compare the Unity tutorial with my engine. Give one small test. Separate facts from assumptions. Do not replace the whole project.”

No live AI? Check this supplied answer: “A scene object can carry behavior. Its script or event reads data and responds to an input or lifecycle event. Changing a number in the editor is not proof of a working interaction; run a test and inspect the result. Unity’s component APIs and another engine’s node/actor APIs can differ.” Verify one statement in the official docs, test it and explain what you kept or corrected.

Communication / 30 points

Every person explains every required part clearly and accurately. Use your notes and open the actual files. Sharing tasks does not divide the required understanding.

  • Purpose and plan / 8 points: Explain the player, backstory, goal, plan and why you chose this engine.
  • Technical understanding / 8 points: Open the actual project. Explain folders, scene/object structure, actions, data and relevant C# concepts or engine equivalents.
  • Evidence and questions / 8 points: Use notes/screenshots to explain what you tried, what happened, why, and what you will test next.
  • Whole-project handoff / 6 points: Explain all required parts, who did what, current status and the next-class/final plan. Answer follow-up questions clearly.

All ★ evidence must be present. A missing starred item may cap the score at 1 point under the syllabus. The instructor verifies the work and records the grade.

  1. Walk through the whole project. Explain context, plan, folders, scene, player, assets, behavior/code, data, tests and result. Show the evidence as you speak.
  2. Answer questions. Use your documentation. Say what you know, locate the proof and explain an uncertainty or failure with a next test.
  3. Compare the work. Keep a concise Discord/peer-check record and update tasks/owners/status. Name the contribution you made and what the group combined.
  4. Plan the next class. Record remaining issues, next improvements, owners and first test. This plan is part of today’s handoff.
